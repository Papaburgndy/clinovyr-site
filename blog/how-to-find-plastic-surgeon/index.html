How to Find the Best Plastic Surgeon Near You (2026 Vetting Guide)

Updated April 20, 2026 · 14 min read

Choosing a plastic surgeon is one of the most consequential health-related decisions you will ever make. Unlike a routine checkup, plastic surgery permanently alters your body, and the outcome depends almost entirely on the skill, training, and judgment of the surgeon you select. A great result can transform your confidence and quality of life. A poor one can mean corrective procedures, lasting complications, and significant emotional distress.

Yet the process of finding the right surgeon is confusing for most people. The industry is loosely regulated when it comes to advertising. Any licensed physician can legally market themselves as a "cosmetic surgeon," regardless of whether they completed a dedicated plastic surgery residency. Online reviews can be misleading. And cost varies so dramatically from city to city that it is difficult to know whether you are getting a fair price or overpaying for mediocre care.

This guide walks you through everything you need to know: how to verify board certification, what red flags to watch for, the questions you should ask during a consultation, what procedures actually cost across the country, and how to evaluate surgeons in five major U.S. cities. Whether you are considering a minimally invasive procedure like fillers or a full surgical transformation, this framework will help you make an informed, confident decision.

Board Certification: The Non-Negotiable

Board certification is the single most important credential to verify before you even schedule a consultation. It is the line that separates surgeons who have completed years of rigorous, specialized training from those who have not. Here is what you need to understand.

ABPS vs. ABCS: Know the Difference

The American Board of Plastic Surgery (ABPS) is the only plastic surgery board recognized by the American Board of Medical Specialties (ABMS). To earn ABPS certification, a surgeon must complete a minimum of six years of surgical training—including a dedicated plastic surgery residency—then pass comprehensive written and oral examinations. ABPS-certified surgeons must also fulfill ongoing continuing education requirements to maintain their certification.

The American Board of Cosmetic Surgery (ABCS) is a separate organization that is not recognized by the ABMS. While some ABCS-certified surgeons are highly skilled, the certification pathways differ significantly. Surgeons certified only by the ABCS may have completed residencies in other specialties—such as dermatology, emergency medicine, or general surgery—before transitioning to cosmetic procedures. This does not necessarily make them unqualified, but it does mean their plastic surgery training may be less extensive.

How to verify: Go to abplasticsurgery.org and use the "Is My Surgeon Certified?" tool. You can also search the ABMS directory at certificationmatters.org. Both are free, public resources. Do this before every consultation—no exceptions.

What Else to Verify

  • Hospital privileges: Even if your procedure will be performed in an outpatient surgical center, a surgeon with active hospital privileges has been independently vetted by a hospital credentialing committee.
  • State medical license: Search your state's medical board website to confirm the license is active and to check for any disciplinary actions, malpractice settlements, or restrictions.
  • Facility accreditation: The surgical facility should be accredited by AAAASF, AAAHC, or have Medicare certification. Accreditation means the facility meets strict safety and equipment standards.
  • Fellowship training: For specialized procedures (craniofacial, microsurgery, hand surgery), look for surgeons who completed additional fellowship training beyond their residency.

Red Flags When Choosing a Plastic Surgeon

The plastic surgery industry is competitive, and not all marketing practices are ethical. Here are the warning signs that should make you reconsider a surgeon or clinic.

Walk away if you encounter any of these:

  1. Pressure to book immediately. "This price is only good today" or "We have one slot left this month" are sales tactics, not medical practice. A reputable surgeon will never rush you into a decision.
  2. Vague answers about credentials. If the surgeon or staff cannot clearly explain their board certification, residency training, or complication rates, that is a serious concern.
  3. No before-and-after gallery. An experienced surgeon will have an extensive gallery of their own patients. Be wary of stock photos or a gallery with very few examples.
  4. Unusually low pricing. If a quote is dramatically below the national average, question what corners are being cut. Are they using a board-certified anesthesiologist? Is the facility accredited? Are follow-up visits included?
  5. The surgeon does not perform the consultation. You should meet and speak directly with the surgeon who will operate on you. Delegating the entire consultation to a patient coordinator is a red flag.
  6. No discussion of risks. Every surgical procedure carries risks. A surgeon who only talks about benefits and avoids discussing potential complications is not providing informed consent.
  7. Negative online patterns. A single bad review can happen to anyone, but a pattern of complaints about the same issues (poor communication, unexpected charges, botched results) should be taken seriously.

10 Questions to Ask During Your Consultation

A consultation is not just about the surgeon evaluating you. It is your opportunity to evaluate the surgeon. Come prepared with these questions and pay attention to how thoroughly they are answered.

  1. Are you certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery? Confirm in person what you already verified online.
  2. How many times have you performed this specific procedure? Volume matters. You want a surgeon who performs your procedure regularly, not occasionally.
  3. What is your complication rate for this procedure? An honest surgeon will have this data and share it transparently.
  4. Can I see before-and-after photos of patients with a similar starting point? Results on a different body type or skin tone may not be representative of what you can expect.
  5. What type of anesthesia will be used, and who administers it? A board-certified anesthesiologist or CRNA should be involved for any procedure requiring general anesthesia or deep sedation.
  6. Where will the surgery be performed, and is the facility accredited? Know the location and its safety credentials.
  7. What does the total cost include? Ask for an itemized quote covering surgeon fees, anesthesia, facility fees, post-operative garments, and follow-up visits.
  8. What is the recovery timeline, and what restrictions will I have? Get specific answers about time off work, physical activity, and when you will see final results.
  9. What happens if I experience a complication or am dissatisfied with the result? Understand the revision policy and any associated costs before you commit.
  10. Can I speak with former patients? Some surgeons can connect you with past patients who have agreed to share their experience. This is invaluable.

Your Plastic Surgeon Vetting Checklist

  • ABPS board certification verified on abplasticsurgery.org
  • Active state medical license with no disciplinary actions
  • Hospital privileges at an accredited institution
  • Accredited surgical facility (AAAASF, AAAHC, or Medicare-certified)
  • Extensive before-and-after gallery with patients similar to you
  • Transparent discussion of risks, complications, and complication rates
  • Itemized cost breakdown provided in writing
  • Clear revision and complication policy
  • At least two to three consultations completed for comparison
  • No high-pressure sales tactics or "limited-time" pricing
  • Positive patterns in online reviews across multiple platforms
  • Board-certified anesthesiologist or CRNA for procedures under sedation

How Much Does Plastic Surgery Cost? National Averages (2026)

Price should never be the primary factor in choosing a surgeon, but understanding the market helps you identify outliers—both suspiciously cheap and unreasonably expensive. The figures below reflect surgeon fees, anesthesia, and facility costs combined.

Procedure National Average Typical Range
Rhinoplasty $7,500 $5,000 – $15,000
Breast Augmentation $6,500 $4,000 – $12,000
Facelift $12,000 $8,000 – $25,000
Tummy Tuck $9,000 $6,000 – $15,000
Liposuction $5,500 $3,000 – $10,000
Blepharoplasty (Eyelid Surgery) $4,500 $2,500 – $7,000

These numbers represent all-inclusive costs in most cases. However, always request an itemized breakdown. Some quotes exclude anesthesia, facility fees, or post-operative garments and compression wear. Financing options like CareCredit and Alphaeon are widely accepted if cost is a barrier.

Finding a Plastic Surgeon in Major U.S. Cities

Location significantly impacts both cost and the pool of available surgeons. Here is what you should know about five of the most popular markets for plastic surgery in the United States.

New York City

New York is one of the most expensive markets for plastic surgery in the country, with costs typically running 20–40% above national averages. A rhinoplasty in Manhattan may cost $10,000–$18,000, while a facelift can exceed $30,000 with a top-tier surgeon. The upside is an extremely deep bench of ABPS-certified surgeons, many affiliated with major academic medical centers like NYU Langone, Mount Sinai, and NewYork-Presbyterian. Focus your search on surgeons with hospital privileges at these institutions. Beware of "boutique" clinics in trendy neighborhoods that prioritize aesthetics and marketing over credentials.

Los Angeles

LA is the plastic surgery capital of the United States by volume. The market is saturated with providers, which means competition can work in your favor on pricing—but it also means a higher proportion of under-qualified practitioners trading on celebrity culture. Expect costs 15–30% above national averages in Beverly Hills and West LA, with more moderate pricing in the San Fernando Valley and outlying areas. Look for surgeons affiliated with UCLA, Cedars-Sinai, or USC. Be especially diligent about verifying credentials in a market where Instagram follower counts are sometimes treated as a proxy for surgical skill.

Miami

Miami has emerged as a global destination for body contouring procedures, particularly Brazilian Butt Lifts (BBLs), tummy tucks, and liposuction. Pricing can be deceptively low compared to New York or LA, with tummy tucks starting around $6,500 and BBLs as low as $5,000. However, the city has also been linked to a disproportionate number of surgical complications and fatalities, particularly at high-volume "chop shop" clinics that prioritize throughput over patient safety. Vet Miami surgeons more aggressively than anywhere else. Confirm accreditation, check complication histories, and consider surgeons affiliated with the University of Miami or Jackson Memorial. For more detail, see our guide to the best plastic surgeons in Miami.

Chicago

Chicago offers a strong balance of quality and affordability. Costs tend to track close to national averages, with some procedures running 5–10% below what you would pay on either coast. The city has an excellent concentration of ABPS-certified surgeons, many associated with Northwestern Memorial, Rush University Medical Center, and the University of Chicago. The Gold Coast and Streeterville neighborhoods are popular clinic locations. Chicago is also a strong market for reconstructive surgery given the academic medical infrastructure in the area.

Houston

Houston is one of the best value markets for plastic surgery in the country. Costs often fall 10–20% below national averages, driven by lower overhead and a competitive provider landscape. The Texas Medical Center—the largest medical complex in the world—is home to Baylor College of Medicine and the University of Texas Health Science Center, both of which produce and attract highly trained surgeons. Houston is particularly strong for breast augmentation and body contouring. Look for surgeons with privileges at Methodist, Memorial Hermann, or the Texas Medical Center hospitals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I verify a plastic surgeon is board certified?
Visit the American Board of Plastic Surgery (ABPS) website at abplasticsurgery.org and use their verification tool. Enter the surgeon's name to confirm active certification. You can also check the American Board of Medical Specialties (ABMS) directory at certificationmatters.org. Only ABPS certification indicates the surgeon completed an accredited plastic surgery residency and passed rigorous written and oral examinations.
How many consultations should I get before choosing?
Most experts recommend consulting with at least two to three board-certified plastic surgeons before making a decision. This allows you to compare surgical approaches, communication styles, facility standards, and pricing. Each consultation typically costs between $100 and $300, though many surgeons apply this fee toward your procedure if you book with them.
What's the difference between a cosmetic surgeon and a plastic surgeon?
A board-certified plastic surgeon has completed a dedicated plastic surgery residency (typically six or more years of surgical training) and is certified by the American Board of Plastic Surgery. The term "cosmetic surgeon" is not tied to a recognized board certification in the same way. Any licensed physician can legally call themselves a cosmetic surgeon, regardless of their training background. Always verify ABPS certification for the highest standard of training.
Does insurance cover plastic surgery?
Insurance generally does not cover elective cosmetic procedures. However, reconstructive plastic surgery—such as breast reconstruction after mastectomy, rhinoplasty to correct breathing issues, or eyelid surgery that impairs vision—may be partially or fully covered. Contact your insurance provider before your consultation and ask the surgeon's office to submit a pre-authorization request if the procedure has a functional or medical component.
How do I evaluate before and after photos?
Look for photos taken under consistent lighting and angles. Focus on patients with a similar body type, skin tone, and aesthetic goals as yours. Ask whether the photos are of the surgeon's own patients and not stock images. Look for natural-looking results, symmetry, and minimal visible scarring. A strong gallery will include photos from multiple stages of recovery, not just the final result.
What should I expect during recovery?
Recovery varies significantly by procedure. Most patients can expect swelling, bruising, and discomfort for the first one to two weeks. Minor procedures like blepharoplasty may have a one-week recovery, while a full facelift or tummy tuck may require three to six weeks before returning to normal activity. Your surgeon should provide a detailed recovery timeline, activity restrictions, and information about post-operative follow-up appointments before you commit to the procedure.

Making Your Decision

Finding the right plastic surgeon is a process, not a snap decision. Start with credentials—ABPS certification is non-negotiable. Then narrow your options by scheduling consultations, evaluating before-and-after galleries, and asking the hard questions about complication rates and revision policies. Compare costs, but never let price be the deciding factor when the outcome is permanent.

The best outcomes come from patients who do their homework. You are already doing that by reading this guide. Use the vetting checklist above, consult with at least two or three surgeons, and trust your instincts about the quality of communication and transparency you experience during those meetings.
